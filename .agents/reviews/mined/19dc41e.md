## What the commit says

> Two independent adversarial reviews (Opus, Fable) of 8099677 -- the device half of
> td-0bcab4 -- confirmed the bearer-token bypass closed, then found the same defect
> class reproduced twice more and five stale claims in DESIGN.
>
> The device half's anti-recurrence measure was making `expected_nats_fp` a REQUIRED
> argument, so skipping the binding is a compile error. That works in Rust because
> `&Fingerprint` is a 32-byte newtype. It does not survive the language boundary: the
> TS twin took a `Uint8Array` and compared with a `bytesEqual` that is vacuously true
> for two empty arrays, so a well-signed attestation naming `nats_fp = []`, checked by
> a caller passing `[]`, "verified" a binding it never compared. Demonstrated accepted
> before and rejected after.
>
> `AdminCommand.signer_fp` was the same shape untouched: in the signed preimage,
> compared by nobody... Latent in both languages -- no production caller exists yet.
>
> `wire_message_is_uniform_across_every_refusal_reason` covered 7 of 17 variants and
> silently accepted the 18th. It is now compiler-enforced.
>
> Separately: pg_store's contract tests TRUNCATE six tables against whatever
> TEST_DATABASE_URL names, with nothing constraining the target -- and deploy/README
> documented pointing it at the live stack's own database.

## Reading

Two independent reviewers, having just confirmed the primary bearer-token bypass from 8099677 was genuinely closed, went looking for the same defect class elsewhere and found it twice more: the TypeScript twin of the session-attestation binding was defeated by an empty-array vacuous-equality bug that Rust's newtype system structurally prevented, and `AdminCommand.signer_fp` carried the identical unenforced-binding shape (currently latent only because no production caller exists yet to exploit it). A third, unrelated finding — a coverage test that silently tolerated a missing case for 10 of 17 enum variants — is the same "unverified coverage claim" pattern seen repeatedly in the vfs-connection-poisoning tickets, here surfacing in a completely different subsystem. The pg_store TRUNCATE-target finding is graver in consequence (an unguarded test suite that could wipe a live database) but the commit introduces it with only "Separately:" and no stated source, so it is recorded here with an explicit note that its provenance (review vs. self-discovery) is ambiguous.
