## What the commit says

> `decide_device_connect` verified the device certificate and every capability,
> but never exercised the device's own identity key. `verify_nkey_sig()` proves
> possession of whichever nkey is presenting — the attacker's own. DESIGN named
> two mechanisms that should have prevented this and neither existed in code:
> `DeviceCertificate.nats_fp` was on the wire and read by no verifier in either
> language, and `sig_device(nats_fp, ts)` had never been implemented at all.
>
> So `{root_pk, device_cert, caps}` was a pure bearer token: anyone who obtained
> a copy connected as that member from any nkey and inherited its full
> permissions (`client_member_permissions`).
>
> Proven live against the composed stack, both directions. With both checks
> neutered, an attacker's freshly generated nkey presenting a stolen bundle —
> including the victim's own genuine attestation — was ADMITTED. Restored: all
> 6 live_signaling tests pass. The unit-level neuter showed `Authorized` with
> full member permissions for a forged attestation signature.

## Reading

This is the device-side twin of 7903370's host-side finding, and equally severe: the entire device bundle (`{root_pk, device_cert, caps}`) functioned as a bearer token because the two mechanisms DESIGN had specified for binding a session to a device's own identity key — a `nats_fp` field and a `sig_device` attestation — existed only on paper. Anyone who obtained a copy of a legitimate member's bundle (not the device's private key) could connect as that member from any nkey and inherit its full permission set. As with the host-side finding, this reads as the implementer's own audit uncovering a defect that had shipped and remained unexercised for as long as the DESIGN requirement had gone unimplemented, proven live by neutering the new checks and observing admission on the real composed stack.
