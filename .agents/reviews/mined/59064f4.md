## What the commit says

> **§A5 gains `helper.revoke.<nfp>`.** §A4 has always said a person revokes a
> lost device with a root-signed revocation "deposited at the helper so the
> callout refuses it even while hosts are offline (S14)", but no subject
> existed to deposit it on: `registry.revoke.<hfp>` is host-only, and the
> client permission list had no revocation subject at all. S14 was
> undeliverable as written.

## Reading

DESIGN.md had, for some number of prior versions, described a client-initiated self-revocation flow (S14: a person revokes their own lost device by depositing a signed revocation at the helper) without ever giving the client any NATS subject it was permitted to publish that revocation on — the only revocation subject that existed (`registry.revoke.<hfp>`) was host-only. As written, the described capability could not be exercised by the actor (a person, not a host) the design claimed it was for. This is a clean `spec-cannot-execute` case, same shape as efb177c9/404b52f2's session-key formula elsewhere in this range: a design section stated a capability that, when actually traced against the permission model it depends on, had no path to work.
