## What the commit says

> Two defects found by an independent review of d40fea7.
>
> `Audit::sign_head` wrote on the shared connection with no poison check and no
> transaction of its own, so on a stranded connection its INSERT ran inside that
> transaction and returned Ok with a real signature:
>
>     sign_head on poisoned store    -> Ok(SignedHead { seq: 1, ... })
>     verify_head after drop+reopen  -> Err(NoSignedHead(1))
>     signed_heads rows after reopen -> 0
>
> 618318a's census claimed "all 26 write paths". It counted `Store`'s 26 mutators
> plus `Audit::append`. `Audit` has two writes on the shared connection, and the
> census never left the `Store` type's boundary — the enumeration was scoped to a
> type when the thing at risk is the connection.
>
> Second, `Store::with_transaction` ran recovery after its closure returned, which
> a panic unwinds straight past:
>
>     after panic: is_autocommit=false is_poisoned=false
>     bump_cap_epoch() after panic -> Ok(1)
>
> The same report-success-while-undurable signature the wrapper exists to close.
> The doc comment claimed recovery ran "after `f` returns by any path" and called
> it airtight, which was false, so the claim is now made true rather than softened.

## Reading

This is the third round of the same connection-poisoning ticket (td-c9b9bd), and both findings repeat the pattern already seen in 618318a and d40fea7: a coverage claim about "all write paths" or "any exit path" was stated more broadly than the code actually guaranteed, and both times a fresh independent review found a concrete case that reproduced the exact original symptom (a write that reports success but is silently lost on reopen). The first defect shows the census enumerating write paths by scanning the `Store` type's methods when the real unit of risk was the shared SQLite *connection*, which `Audit` also writes to directly. The second shows a doc comment asserting "airtight" coverage of a panic-unwind case with no test actually panicking inside the wrapped closure to prove it — the same failure of an *unverified coverage claim* recurring at a different layer (this time a doc comment's own guarantee, rather than a test's).
