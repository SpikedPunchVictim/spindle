## What the commit says

> F1. "All three must move together or none of them can." False. Perturbing
> MEASURED_MEMBER_CAP_BYTES 424 -> 425:
>
>     PASSED=867 FAILED=2
>       artifacts::capability::tests::keeps_measured_member_cap_bytes_honest
>       authorize::tests::production_issuer_mints_caps_at_exactly_measured_...
>
> Two, not three. spindle-helper pinned its own private literals (14_115,
> 18_820) and never read the shared constant.
>
> F2. "Reddens ... for any other drift in a capability's encoded width." False.
> The fixture injects its clock, so `wall_clock_now_secs` was unreachable from
> the pin -- the same shape of gap as d2090dd's, one axis over. Changing
> `.as_secs()` to `.as_millis()` makes every `exp` a 9-byte CBOR uint instead
> of 5 (428 B/cap) and left the suite at 869/0/17.

## Reading

This is the third consecutive rejection in the same ticket lineage, and the review explicitly separated the mechanism from the prose: the pin c312e7c added was sound, but two of the commit's own doc-comment claims about what that pin guaranteed were unverified and, on perturbation, false. Both perturbations are concrete and reproduced in the commit: bumping the shared constant by one byte broke two tests, not the claimed three, revealing that `spindle-helper` had its own unlinked private literals; and switching the production clock's time unit — the exact kind of "other drift" the pin claimed to catch — left the entire suite green. The commit's closing observation names the pattern precisely: "across v0.9.33, v0.9.34 and v0.9.35 the same failure repeats at decreasing scale -- each fix described a guarantee stronger than the one it built."
