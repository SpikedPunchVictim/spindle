## What the commit says

> `VfsRpcServer::handle` checked member status unconditionally but ran the
> device-revocation check inside `if let Some(device_fp) = ctx.device_fp`, so
> a context with `device_fp: None` was served without device revocation ever
> being evaluated. Note the asymmetry it left: a `Some` naming a device the
> member does not have already denied (`denied:unknown_device`). Only `None`
> failed open.
>
> That gate is a compensating control, not a nice-to-have. DESIGN.md:330 calls
> the host's per-request rejection of revoked keys authoritative, and
> DESIGN.md:1022 records that the helper's callout epoch check was deliberately
> demoted to best-effort *because* of it. A conditional compensating control is
> a thinner guarantee than the design describes.
>
> Not exploitable in the current build — VfsSessionHandler::session_context is
> the sole production constructor and always supplied a fingerprint (every
> SessionContext construction site in the workspace was enumerated to confirm
> it). But SessionContext is `pub` and re-exported, and per DESIGN §A10.26 the
> Stage 7 Tauri shell runs the daemon in-process, so it constructs these
> directly. Fail-open by omission, waiting for a caller.

## Reading

The device-revocation check — described in DESIGN.md as the authoritative,
per-request enforcement point that the rest of the system's more relaxed
checks are allowed to lean on — was implemented as conditional on
`device_fp` being `Some`, so any `SessionContext` constructed with `None`
would skip device-revocation evaluation entirely and be served: a fail-open
by omission rather than by mistake in the check's logic itself. The
implementer is careful to note this was not exploitable in the shipped build
because the one production constructor always supplies a fingerprint, but
the type is `pub` and a documented future caller (the Stage 7 Tauri shell)
would construct `SessionContext` directly and could trip it. This traces to
the 2026-09-02 bug hunt (td-de94c5) and fits `missing-guard` cleanly: the
same invariant enforced on one branch of a match and silently absent on its
sibling.
