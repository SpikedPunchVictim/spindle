## What the commit says

> DESIGN.md §A4 makes the host the *authoritative* revocation check, per
> request, over keys named as `root_fp | device_fp`. Only the `root_fp` half
> was implemented: `VfsRpcServer::handle` gated on `member.status` and never
> read `Device.revoked`, so revoking one device of a member who stays Active
> revoked nothing at all — that device kept full entitlement.

> identity is absent by construction.

## Reading

The VFS RPC server's per-request revocation check — the enforcement point DESIGN.md names as authoritative — only ever checked half of its stated key (`root_fp`, via `member.status`) and never read the `Device.revoked` flag that had existed in the store since Stage 6 slice 1. In practice this meant revoking a single device belonging to a still-active member had no effect at all: that device kept full entitlement indefinitely. It shipped silently in Stage 6 slice 3 (1d31ad21) and stayed that way because the entire negative-test harness constructs every request context with `device_fp: None`, so no existing test could ever have exercised the device-scoped path even by accident. This is a missing-guard defect in the strictest sense: the invariant (revocation is authoritative) was enforced for one half of its stated key and silently absent for the other.
