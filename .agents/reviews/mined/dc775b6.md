## What the commit says

> HostOpKeyCert carried `nats_fp`, but `verify_host_op_key_cert` never read it.
> The field was a signed binding that no verifier checked — td-0bcab4's defect
> class, one artifact over. The only thing enforcing the host's per-connect NATS
> binding was a single manual field comparison at one call site in
> decide_host_connect, which inspects bytes and never a signature.
>
> The certificate was serving two unrelated roles: the issuance chain embedded in
> every Capability, and the host's per-connect CONNECT credential. Split them.
>
> The new binding is closed by construction, not by a check: a member holding a
> capability has host_root_pk and op_cert but never the operating private key, so
> it cannot forge a valid attestation for any nkey.
>
> Proven live, not just green. A new live test has a real member decode
> host_root_pk and op_cert out of its own genuine capability and CONNECT with a
> self-forged attestation naming its own nkey — self-consistent enough that the
> cheap pre-check passes, so only the authoritative check can refuse it. With
> both checks removed and the helper image rebuilt, the live stack ADMITTED that
> connect; with them restored it refuses. Live suite 6/6.

## Reading

Having just fixed the same unenforced-binding-field defect on the device side and (in 7903370) partially on the host side, the implementer's own follow-up audit found a third instance one artifact over: `HostOpKeyCert.nats_fp` was a signed field that no verifier ever read, with only a byte comparison at a single call site standing in for real enforcement. This is a distinct instance from 7903370's finding — that commit added the missing byte comparison against the cert; this commit discovers the underlying certificate was structurally unsound for the role (mixing an issuance-chain artifact with a per-connect credential) and replaces the field-based binding with a domain-separated, per-session attestation signed by the operating key, closing the binding "by construction" rather than by another checkable field. The live-admission proof (self-forged attestation, real member capability, real docker-compose stack) again demonstrates the defect was genuinely exploitable, not theoretical.
